# Family usability pilot plan

## Purpose

Learn whether children can complete a micro-lesson, understand the feedback and use the explanation controls. This is a usability and content review, not a clinical study and not proof that adaptation improves learning outcomes.

## Preconditions

Do not recruit or collect research data until the responsible organization has reviewed applicable requirements, the guardian information and consent process, age-appropriate child assent, facilitator guidance, and the handling of notes or recordings. The prototype itself does not need a child account and should not be modified to collect personal data for the pilot.

## Suggested first round

- 5–8 child–guardian pairs, recruited voluntarily through an appropriate school or family network.
- Include the intended age bands only if the team can provide age-appropriate supervision and review.
- One facilitator observes; the guardian remains available, but should not answer on the child's behalf.
- 15–20 minutes per pair, with a clear option to stop at any time.
- Use a local copy of the prototype; do not ask families to enter names, school details or other identifying information.

This small round is for finding usability problems, not estimating population-wide outcomes.

## Tasks

1. Find a subject and topic.
2. Choose a preferred explanation format.
3. Explain in their own words what the learning objective means.
4. Answer the question without a hint, then try a hint if appropriate.
5. Read the feedback and decide what to do next.
6. Ask the guardian to interpret the parent summary.
7. Reset the session and confirm that the displayed progress clears.

## Observe

Record task completion and broad issue categories rather than identifying details:

- Could the child find the controls without help?
- Did the child understand the example, hint and answer explanation?
- Did the child understand that explanation formats can be changed?
- Did the guardian interpret the session result as a limited observation rather than an ability score?
- Were there keyboard, screen-reader, contrast, language or reading-load barriers?
- Did any wording create shame, pressure or a fixed label?

Avoid recording audio/video or retaining verbatim child statements unless a separately reviewed protocol requires it.

## Success criteria for the next iteration

- No blocker prevents a child from completing the main task.
- Children can describe the feedback as being about the answer, not their intelligence or personality.
- Guardians understand the limits of the summary.
- Every critical accessibility or safety issue has an owner and resolution plan.
- Repeated confusion is converted into a specific product or content change.

## Learning-outcome evaluation is separate

Do not claim improved learning outcomes from satisfaction or completion alone. A later evaluation should define an age-appropriate assessment, comparison condition, delayed recall measure, analysis plan and subgroup-fairness checks before collecting data. Obtain appropriate research and legal review first.
